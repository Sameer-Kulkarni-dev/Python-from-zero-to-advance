 Get Slice>> O(k)

 The Group Photo Analogy
Imagine a massive line of 1,000 people waiting for a movie.
 You decide you want a separate group photo of the people standing in positions 10 through 20
 .To do this, you have to walk up to each of those 10 specific people 
 and pull a copy of them over to a brand-new line for the photo.
 If you only want a photo of 2 people, it is very fast. If you want a photo of 500 people,
  it takes a lot longer. The time it takes depends entirely on the size of the new group (k). 

  The Golden Rule to Memorize:"Slicing isn't just looking; it is copying.
   Copying k items takes O(k) time."
 ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 1. Delete Slice: del my_list[2:5] -> O(n)
 What Python is Actually Doing:
Python deletes that chunk of items.
 Then, to keep the list as a solid block of memory without holes,
 it grabs every single item to the right of the deleted chunk and shifts them left.
 The time it takes depends on how many items are left over that need to be moved O(n)
 ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 2. Set Slice: my_list[2:5] = ["A", "B", "C", "D"] -> O(n + k)
What Python is Actually Doing:
Setting a slice is a two-part job for Python:Make room (The O(n) part): 
If the new list of items is a different size than the old chunk,
Python must shift all the remaining items in the list to the right (to make more room)
or to the left (to close extra space).

Put the new items in (The O(k) part): Python then copies the new items (k) into that perfectly sized gap.
The Golden Rule to Memorize:"If you change the size of a gap in the middle of a list,
 everyone behind the gap has to shift (O(n))."
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Extend: my_list.extend(new_list) -> O(k) Where k is the number of items in the new_list you are adding.
The Movie Line Analogy:
A bus pulls up and 20 new people get off to join the very back of the movie line.
 The original people in the line don't have to move or shift at all.
 The time it takes just depends on how long it takes those 20 new people (k) to walk off the bus and
  stand at the end.
  
  What Python is Actually Doing:
Python takes the items from the new list and tacks them onto the end of the original list, one by one.
Because they are being added to the back, there is no shifting. It simply takes $k$ steps to copy k items.
